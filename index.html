How the ledger decides

Day count

Days outside India = days between passport exit and entry, plus every day from CDC sign-on to CDC sign-off on a foreign-going voyage. For a voyage that starts or ends at an Indian port, the CDC period is excluded from your India stay under Rule 126 (FY 2025-26 and earlier) and Rule 8 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026 with s.6 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (tax year 2026-27 onwards).

Days in India = days in the year − days outside. Keep it at 181 or fewer: at least 184 days outside in a 365-day year, 185 in a leap year.

Resident or not

Resident if in India 182 days or more. An Indian citizen working abroad or as crew gets 182 days in place of the 60-day test.

If your Indian income (excluding foreign income) is over ₹15 lakh, a stay of 120–181 days with 365+ days in the previous 4 years makes you resident (RNOR). Not applied in the year you first leave India for employment.

Deemed resident (RNOR): Indian citizen, Indian income over ₹15 lakh, and not liable to tax in any other country. Most seafarers are not tax resident anywhere else, so watch this one.

RNOR or ROR

A resident is RNOR if NR in 9 of the 10 previous years, or in India 729 days or less in the 7 previous years. Otherwise ROR.

NR: salary for service on a foreign-going ship is outside Indian tax, even when credited to your NRE account (CBDT Circular 13/2017). Indian income (FD interest, rent, gains) is still taxable.

RNOR: foreign income is taxed only if received in India. ROR: worldwide income including sea salary is taxable.

This ledger applies the published day-count rules to the dates you enter. Keep your CDC, passport stamps, joining letters and wage slips as proof, and confirm your status with a chartered accountant before filing.
